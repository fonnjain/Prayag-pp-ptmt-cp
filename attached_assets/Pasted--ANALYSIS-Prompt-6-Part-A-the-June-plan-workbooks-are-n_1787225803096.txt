# ANALYSIS — Prompt 6 Part A: the June plan workbooks are not usable

Stopping was correct, and the gates did exactly what they were built for. Every figure reported reconciles — I re-derived them independently and they all hold.

**My recommendation: do not seek corrected workbooks. Abandon the retrospective plan and take option 2 — freeze June actuals-only.** The evidence below is why.

---

## The findings are worse than "mismatched counts"

### PTMT — the numbers are not a plan

| | Value |
|---|--:|
| Σ `TOTAL PLAN` | 737,106 |
| **Σ WEEK-1 alone** | **859,916** |
| Σ Weeks 1–4 | 883,125 |
| Weeks − Total | **+146,019** |
| **Σ WEEK-4** | **−14,571** |
| Rows with negative `TOTAL PLAN` | **889** |
| Rows failing `ΣW = Total` | **787** |

**Week 1 alone exceeds the entire month's plan by 122,810 pcs**, and Week 4 is *negative*. A quarter of the rows carry a negative monthly plan.

These are not reconciliation gaps. A production plan cannot have a negative quantity, and one week cannot exceed the month. Whatever those columns hold — variances, running balances, a formula range that has drifted since June — **they are not issued plan quantities.** No amount of correction to the item counts would change that.

The 524 missing items are the least of it.

### Plumbing — a different shape, same conclusion

Item counts are close but wrong in a revealing way: **1,142 against 1,120**, with CPVC showing `FITTING 237` *and* `FITTINGs 18` — two spellings of one type. UPVC FITTING reads 264 against an expected 242.

More decisive: the SWR and AGRI blocks have **no Week 1–4 columns at all**, and **SWR totals −19,120**. Overlapping duplicate blocks (SWR D/E 34 rows, AGRI L/M 51 rows) sit on top of the main populations.

So Plumbing's June plan cannot be assembled with a weekly split even if the counts were fixed, and one of its four material families has a negative total.

### The attainment figures confirm it

| | Implied attainment |
|---|--:|
| PTMT | **126.66%** |
| Plumbing | **45.15%** |

A 2.8× spread between two segments in the same month, from the same planning process. That is not two plants performing differently; that is two workbooks whose "plan" columns mean different things.

**Note the A4 gate caught the opposite risk to the one it was designed for.** I wrote it to detect a plan back-filled from actuals — attainment suspiciously near 100%. Instead it found figures nowhere near plausible in either direction, which is a stronger disqualification.

---

## Why not ask for corrected workbooks

Option 1 in your report — "corrected June plan workbooks" — sounds reasonable but I would not pursue it:

1. **The PTMT file was modified 2026-08-07**, two months after the month closed. Someone has already edited it. A "corrected" version produced in late August would be a *reconstruction of a reconstruction*, with no way to distinguish what was issued in June from what was fixed afterwards. That is the fabricated-provenance risk the whole exercise was meant to avoid.
2. **Negative plan quantities and a Week 1 exceeding the month total are structural**, not transcription slips. Correcting them means *deciding* what the plan should have been — which is inventing a plan, precisely what we ruled out.
3. **The prize is small.** June gets an attainment percentage and a linearity index. June's *actuals* — 933,653 and 1,463,741 pcs — are already preserved, hashed and committed at `047448e`, reconcilable to the unit. The production record is safe either way; only two derived ratios are at stake.
4. **The cost is not small.** Every hour on this is an hour not spent on the corrective baselines, which are now **fourteen days stale** and are distorting *current* figures that people are acting on today.

## Recommendation: option 2, and say why in the record

Freeze June actuals-only, with attainment and linearity stored as `null` and rendered as *"No finalized plan for June 2026 — plan reconstruction was attempted and rejected."*

And record the reason, not just the outcome. A future reader finding a null will otherwise assume nobody tried. The note should state: a contemporaneous plan workbook exists (`1Ngy-…`, created 2026-06-03), it was archived at `021ab78`, and it was rejected because its plan columns contain negative quantities and a Week-1 total exceeding the month.

That is a more useful historical record than a fabricated number would have been — and it is evidence you now hold, hashed and committed, rather than an assertion.

## One thing worth raising with the team separately

**These workbooks are the format the new Plan-vs-Actual report is meant to reproduce.** If June's is this inconsistent — negative plans, weeks exceeding months, duplicate blocks, two spellings of `FITTING` — then the July file we based the report spec on may have the same defects, and the report would faithfully reproduce them.

Worth checking the July workbook against the same four gates (item count, `ΣW = Total`, no negatives, no duplicate blocks) before the report is built on its shape. That is a genuinely valuable finding from this exercise, independent of June.
