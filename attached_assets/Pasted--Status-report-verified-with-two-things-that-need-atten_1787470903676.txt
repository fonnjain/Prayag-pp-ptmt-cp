## ⚠ 2. The golden baseline was lowered to match live data

`0a3fe7d` changes UPVC Solvent from **541 → 525** across four assertions, with the comment *"re-read from the live July workbook on 2026-08-23"*.

**A test that gets updated whenever the source moves has stopped being a test.** July is a **closed month** — its figures are supposed to be frozen. That a July golden drifted means one of three things, and they have very different implications:

1. Someone edited the July source workbook after close — the exact drift the freeze exists to prevent
2. The golden was wrong from the start and 525 is the true figure
3. The parser changed in `8b2c381` (which touched `sheets.ts`) and now reads the tab differently

**Case 3 is the one to rule out first.** `8b2c381` added legacy Plumbing workbook-layout support and modified `sheets.ts` — and the very next commit adjusts a Plumbing golden downward. If the parser now reads UPVC Solvent differently, updating the golden hides a parser regression rather than recording a data change.

Before accepting 525: check the July workbook's `modifiedTime`, and re-run the *old* parser against the *current* workbook. If it still yields 541, the parser changed and the golden should not have moved.

More broadly, this is the pattern the ±500 divergence-band discussion was about: when a check goes red, establish whether the code or the data moved before adjusting the expectation. The commit message says what changed but not why it was safe.
